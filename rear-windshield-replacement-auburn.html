What Does Rear Windshield Replacement Involve?

Back glass is tempered, so when it breaks it falls into the trunk, the cargo area and the back seat. The technician vacuums all of it out first, then removes the trim and cuts away what is left of the old adhesive. The new rear windshield is set in fresh urethane, the defroster and antenna connectors are plugged back in, and on hatchbacks and SUVs the rear wiper is refitted. Pickup sliding back windows are a separate part with their own track and latch.

Why Does a Rear Windshield Shatter All at Once?

A rear windshield is tempered glass, like the side windows, and not the laminated glass used in the front. Tempered glass is heat-treated so that any break releases all the stress in the pane at once, and it crumbles into small cubes. Cold makes it touchier. A hatch slammed on a freezing morning, with the rear defroster heating one side of the glass, puts a sudden shock into the pane. People ask, “Why did it break when nothing hit it?” A tiny edge chip from years ago can be enough.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a rear windshield be repaired instead of replaced?

No. Rear windshields are tempered glass, so once they crack or break they cannot be filled with resin like a front windshield chip. The whole pane has to be replaced.

Will my rear defroster work after the new glass goes in?

Yes. New rear glass comes with its own defroster grid, and the technician reconnects the power leads and checks that the grid heats before leaving.

How long does rear windshield replacement take?

Most rear windshields take about an hour, plus time to vacuum out the broken glass. The adhesive needs a short cure before you drive.

Do you replace sliding rear windows on pickups?

Yes. Manual and power sliding back windows on pickups are ordered for your exact truck and installed with the track and latch.
